**Summary.** The project is in the concept phase (milestone M0). Four more milestones, in pre-production, lead to a playtested cage, which is the first version of the vertical slice. Dates wait for the deadlines (Q6); plan at most about 70 percent of the available time.

## Milestones

| Id | Name | Playable goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | Concept: foundations lock | Pitch, pillars, audience and platform approved; open design decisions settled; living GDD validated | Current milestone; concept exit gate below |
| M1 | Slime and mass | Slime body scales with mass; Substrate decision on the slime material | Pre-production; riskiest rendering and art items first |
| M2 | Aspirate, keep, taste, digest | In the greybox cage the player aspirates, keeps, tastes, digests, spits and throws | Pre-production; may reuse the aspiration test components |
| M3 | States and reactions | The four temperature states and property reactions work from data; dirt prototype | Pre-production; core of pillars 1 and 2 |
| M4 | Cage vertical slice and playtest | Cage with one obstacle and two or more solutions, tested by outside players, plan approved | Pre-production exit gate; then the slice at final quality |

## Concept exit gate (current)

- Studio rule: the user approves the pillars and the pitch.
- Proposed for this project: the open design decisions (DEC-03, DEC-06, DEC-08, DEC-04 details; DEC-02 is decided) are settled; the living GDD is validated by the user; the sheets v1 are reviewed against the decisions (T6).
- Checked by the producer (T28); the user decides the move to pre-production.

## Pre-production exit gate

- Studio rule: the core loop is fun in playtests, the top technical risks are resolved, and the plan is approved.
- GDD gate for its first phase (proposed): aspirate, spit and throw already pleasant in one room; one puzzle with two solutions; tested with two people outside the project.
- The cage is built twice: as a greybox prototype first, then at final quality for the vertical slice.
- Prototype code and Blueprints are disposable and labeled as such.
